How this index knows what it knows.

The index separates policy records, quantitative indicators, and source discovery. Each layer has its own verification rules so a reachable link is never mistaken for a current law or a comparable metric.

What “verified” means

The linked official primary source was opened and checked during the stated review. It does not guarantee that no later amendment, judgment, notification, or implementation change exists.

Three evidence layers

Different claims need different checks

Keeping the layers separate prevents a strong source in one part of the index from lending false certainty to another.

01

Policy instruments

Laws, bills, rules, policies, directives, and frameworks are recorded as individual instruments with stable identifiers.

  • Official primary document preferred
  • Dates preserve known precision
  • Legal status stays conservative
  • Unresolved records remain visibly flagged
02

Quantitative indicators

Official observations provide context beside relevant policies, without claiming that a policy caused the measured outcome.

  • Units and reference periods are explicit
  • Revised and provisional values are retained
  • Definition breaks stop trend comparisons
  • Every series exposes its source locator

Verification rules

Certainty is earned one layer at a time.

Verified policy record

The current record URL matches audited evidence and was opened as an official instrument or official publication during the release review.

Unverified policy record

The primary document is inaccessible, the current legal status is unresolved, or the available official evidence cannot support a more definite claim.

Reachable source-hub link

The resource returned acceptable content to the automated audit. Reachability does not establish completeness, currency, or comparability.

Limited source-hub link

The official resource was identified during review but blocks, redirects, or otherwise limits automated verification. The reason remains visible on its card.

Indicator confidence

Confidence describes the evidence—not importance

A low-confidence series can still matter. The label tells readers how much interpretation was required to normalize it.

Indicator confidence levels and evidence standards
Level Evidence standard Reader action
High Defined official table or dataset with clear units, periods, and revision markers. Check the reference period and any provisional or revised flag.
Medium Official values are available, but field definitions, denominators, or revision practice are incomplete. Read the methodology note before comparing values.
Low The measure is a snapshot, proxy, definition-changing series, or requires material interpretation. Use as context only and keep the published limitation attached.
Comparison rules

Missing observations are not zero. Publication dates are not reference periods. Accounts, subscriptions, transactions, devices, and people are not interchangeable. Known definition changes are not drawn as continuous trends.

Refreshes & corrections

A dated snapshot, not a claim of continuous monitoring

Source availability and legal status can change after publication. The visible dates tell readers when evidence was last checked.

  1. 01
    Re-open official sources

    Confirm the destination, publisher, document, and access status.

  2. 02
    Recheck status and definitions

    Look for amendments, superseding instruments, revisions, and methodological breaks.

  3. 03
    Run release contracts

    Validate identifiers, dates, relationships, URLs, units, ordering, and published audit evidence.

  4. 04
    Publish the new snapshot

    Update visible dates, preserve uncertainty, and never silently convert missing evidence into certainty.

Corrections are welcome

A useful correction identifies the record, states the proposed change, and cites an official source. The repository provides the public audit trail for review and revision.

Known limitations

What this release does not claim

  • The index is curated, not exhaustive, and is not legal advice.
  • A verified URL does not prove that an instrument remains legally unchanged.
  • An indicator beside a policy does not establish causation or policy effectiveness.
  • International rankings can embed assumptions that are not specific to Pakistan.
  • Official administrative data may be revised after this snapshot.
  • Automated reachability does not establish methodological suitability.
